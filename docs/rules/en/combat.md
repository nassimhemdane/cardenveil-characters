## Action and Combat

Fights and action phases in this system take place in **rounds**, which represent a unit of time where each player and enemy acts once. During a round, each player has a **turn** to perform their actions: attack, use an ability, move, or use a technique. Once all participating elements have acted, the next round begins.

Abilities are activated using a **deck of cards**, which simulates your resources and tactical decisions. Damage inflicted, on the other hand, is determined by dice rolls.

**Units:** the meter is the primary unit. On a grid or VTT, **1 square = 1.5 m**. The standard zone of control extends **1.5 m** around the creature.

**Standard ranges:** 1.5 m for the zone of control; 3 m for a small range around yourself; 5 m for medium circles and cones; 7 m for extended range; 10 m for a line; 20 m for a long line. A remotely placed circle is 5 m in diameter and may be centered up to 20 m away. A push always moves its target **5 m**, unless explicitly stated otherwise.

## 1. Resources and Action Types

Each character has, per **global round**, the following resources. They all reset **at the start of each global round**:

### 🔹 Action

- Attack, use an ability, interact, perform a significant maneuver, etc.

### 🔹 Bonus Action

- Quick or secondary actions (consumables, Dash, second attack, etc.).

### 🔹 Reaction

- **Only once per global round.**
- Can be played **in reaction to an event**, **even outside your turn** (enemy attack, movement, ability, breach opening, etc.).

### 🔹 Movement

- Total budget per round: **5 + (Agility ÷ 2) m**.
- This budget may be **split into at most 2 segments** during the round, except when using the **Dash Bonus Action**, which allows a third segment; splitting movement does not multiply the Movement budget (for example: a movement during an intervention in GM Phase, then another during your turn in Player Phase).

### 🔹 Hierarchy

> Action ⊃ Bonus Action ⊃ Reaction

- An **Action** may be used for an Action, Bonus Action or Reaction ability.
- A **Bonus Action** may be used for a Bonus Action or Reaction ability, unless a condition prevents it.
- A **Reaction** may only be used for a Reaction ability when its trigger occurs. It can never pay for an Action or Bonus Action.
- Using a Reaction ability with an Action or Bonus Action is not a new Reaction. A character may therefore use several Reaction abilities in one round with different resources while still being limited to **one actual Reaction per round**.

## 2. Initiative and Flow

A character may split their **total Movement budget** into **at most two segments per round**, except when using the **Dash Bonus Action**, which allows a third segment. The combined distance of all segments cannot exceed the round’s budget, increased by Dash where applicable.

### 2.1 GM Phase

The GM describes:

- NPC actions,
- threats,
- environmental effects,
- everything that triggers on the enemy side.

During this phase, players can **intervene**, according to four different modes, with or without an **initiative roll**.

### 🔹 Reaction (Without Initiative)

- Usable **outside your turn**, as soon as the trigger appears.
- **No** initiative is required.
- Does not allow Movement, unless a specific ability states otherwise.

### 🔹 Action or Bonus Action (With Initiative)

The player can attempt to act **before** an NPC's action by making an **initiative roll**.

- **Success** → their Action or Bonus Action resolves **before** the NPC.
- **Failure** → the NPC's action resolves normally.

The player can use their **Movement** during this intervention → **within the limit of two segments per round, or three with the Dash Bonus Action.**

Each player may attempt **only one intervention per NPC turn**, with that turn including all of the NPC’s actions. If the attempt fails, none of the announced resources are spent. The player must wait until at least one NPC has completed its turn before attempting another intervention during a different NPC’s turn.

**Initiative Roll Comparison:**

- either against the NPC's initiative (the one rolled at the start of combat),
- or against a **contextual DC set by the GM** if the event is not linked to an NPC (trap, collapse, environmental danger, cry, lever, etc.).
- On a tie, the **NPC acts first**. The GM may instead give priority to the player if that distributes actions more effectively.

### 🔹 Movement (With Initiative)

The player can intervene solely to **move**: retreat, gain height, reposition, etc.

- Always requires an initiative roll.
- Movement resolves **before** the threat if the initiative is successful.
- Movement counts as **one use of Movement**, → limited to **two segments per round, or three with the Dash Bonus Action**, within the remaining total distance.
- A player can make a Movement intervention even if they plan **no Action or Bonus Action** in the round.

### 🔹 Rush (Reaction)

A special Reaction allowing action **during** an NPC's action.

- Initiative roll (often with advantage according to Rush rules).
- **Success** → the player's Action **and/or** Bonus Action **directly confront** that of the NPC.
- Both actions are resolved **simultaneously**, according to the GM.
- Can include **Movement**, → **within the limit of two segments, or three with the Dash Bonus Action, and the total Movement budget for the round.**

### 2.2 Player Phase

Next, players play out what they **have left** in the round:

- **Action**
- **Bonus Action**
- **Movement** (or what remains)
- **Reaction** (if not used)

They can act **in the order they want**.

If multiple players wish to intervene simultaneously in GM Phase, they freely determine their order.

## 3. Duration of Effects

### 3.1 Effects on a Creature

> An effect "1 turn" on a creature expires only when:
>
> • the target has completed their next turn
>
> **AND**
>
> • the caster has started their next turn.
>
> The effect lasts until the **later of the two events**.
>
> The target’s turn still counts for this duration even if they are unconscious, controlled or unable to act.

### 3.2 Environmental Effects / Zones

> Their duration is counted in global rounds:
>
> • 1 round = until the end of the next global round.
>
> • 2 rounds = until the end of round +2, etc.
>
> Example: a wall of fire placed at round 3 remains active until the end of round 4 (duration 1 round).

## ⚙️ Making an Initiative Roll

1. 🎲 **Roll 1d20.**
2. ➕ **Add your initiative bonus.**
3. 🎯 **Advantage?** Add **+1 die per advantage** (keep the best).
4. 🧾 **Final Result = best d20 + initiative bonus.**

## Available Actions in Combat

In this section, we will present a general idea of possible actions during a round.

### Main Actions

1. **Attack**: Perform an attack with your weapon.
2. **Dodge**: Grants **disadvantage** to enemy attacks against you and **advantage** on saving throws until your next round and **protects** you from opportunity attacks.

### Bonus Actions

1. **Secondary Attack**: A second melee attack without a damage modifier.
2. **Consumable**: Drinking a potion or using a scroll, for example.
3. **Dash**: Increase your movement speed by half, allowing you a third movement during the round.
4. **Imbue**: Imbue a **weapon** or **projectile** with a **consumable.** If an **elemental source** is within range (e.g., fire, toxic substances, etc.), use it to imbue your weapon and add **Nature-Survival Mod** in elemental damage to your next attack.
5. **Equipment Swap**: Alternate between two weapons or equipment.
6. **Stabilization**: As a Bonus Action, revive an unconscious ally. They return to **1 HP**, keep their Fatigue level, and retain only resources they had not already spent this round. Stabilization does not refresh any resource.
7. **Push**: Push back a creature by **5m**, contested by your **Athletics** rolls.
8. **Hide**: If you are **out of sight** or **obscured**, hide yourself by making a **Stealth** roll against enemy **Perception**. At the start of **each round** and every time you **enter a line of sight**, make another roll. Any **hostile** action **reveals** you; a non-hostile action requires a **new roll** to remain hidden.
9. **Analysis**: Make an Investigation roll (DC 10 + Agility Mod) on an opposing creature to discover one of its resistances, immunities, or vulnerabilities. (Or a contextual detail)
10. **Taunt**: make a **Performance** check against a creature’s **Insight**. On a success, it becomes **Provoked** until the end of the round.
11. **Channeling**: Channel your **Focus** and activate its **Suit Bonus** for this round and the next. Abilities of the corresponding suit see their **cost reduced by your Spirit Mod + the Focus bonus**. If you wield two one-handed Foci, you can channel them simultaneously to combine their **suits**. The Focus is then surrounded by an aura of its **damage type**. By channeling, you gain **advantage** on your **attack rolls** with the Focus and **Raw Power (contextual Mod d6)** of abilities.
12. **Flattery**: Make a **Deception** roll against **Ally's Insight**. On success, the ally gains **+ Social Mod** to damage of their next attack.

### Reactions

1. **Opportunity Attack**: If an enemy **leaves** your **Zone of Control**, casts a **ranged** ability, or is **attacked** by an **ally** in **melee**, you may **attack** them (with both weapons if dual wielding). The opportunity attack resolves **before** the movement, casting or attack that triggered it. It may itself trigger opportunity attacks from other allies, forming a chain resolved in order before the original event resumes.
2. **Support**: When an **ally** in your **zone of control** attacks, offer them **advantage** on their attack roll.
3. **Parry**: use your Reaction to reduce damage before Armor. With one weapon: `maximum die ÷ 2 + Agility Mod + Deflection`. With a shield: `maximum shield die ÷ 2 + Strength Mod + Deflection`. With two weapons: `weapon 1 maximum die ÷ 2 + weapon 2 maximum die ÷ 2 + Agility Mod + Deflection`. Add the modifier and Deflection only once.
4. **Bastion**: Attempt to take any attack or effect in place of an ally, or protect an object, with an Acrobatics check against `3 + 2 × distance in meters`. The Reaction is spent even on a failure. Resolve the Bastion check first: on a failure, there is no interception or automatic Parry. On a success against a single-target attack, you take it in the ally’s place and apply your own defenses. With a shield, you automatically Parry without spending an additional resource. Against an area attack (AOE), apply Bastion’s multipliers first: you take **×1.5 damage** and the protected person takes **×0.5 damage**. Each then applies their own immunities, resistances and vulnerabilities, any Parry, and finally Armor. The shield’s automatic Parry protects only you.
5. **Rush**: Use your **Reaction** to make an **initiative roll with advantage** during another creature’s action. The Reaction is spent whether the roll succeeds or fails. If your result exceeds their initiative, you may immediately use your **Action and/or Bonus Action**; they resolve simultaneously with the current action as decided by the GM. If those resources have already been used, roll against **10 + the current creature’s initiative** to borrow the Action and/or Bonus Action from the next round; every borrowed resource is already spent for that future round. This borrowing may be chained during later rounds, each time spending the Reaction and using the increased threshold. Track Action and Bonus Action separately: an available resource is used normally; only an already spent resource must be borrowed. If the same Rush uses both an available resource and a borrowed resource, use the increased threshold. You may only borrow resources from the immediately following round, never from a later round, even if an effect grants you an additional Reaction. An already borrowed resource cannot be borrowed a second time. At the start of the next round, borrowed resources remain spent, but your Reaction resets normally and may be used to borrow from the round after that. The threshold remains **10 + the current creature’s initiative**: the +10 does not accumulate.
6. **Harmonization**: When **another creature within 20 m** uses a **Spirit** ability, use your Reaction to **Amplify: +Spirit Mod** to its Raw Power roll and Save DC, or **Dampen: −Spirit Mod** to its direct damage and Save DC. You cannot target yourself.
7. **Alteration**: When you or an ally uses an ability, you may **adjust** its effect or range without increasing its power and potentially activate an **elemental combination**.
8. **Targeting**: When an enemy acts (attacks, moves, etc.), you can use your **reaction** to **focus on them**, analyzing their movements and granting yourself **advantage** for a shot against them on the next turn.
9. **Dissuasion**: Make an **Intimidation** roll against the **Enemy's Insight** to give them **disadvantage** on their melee attack.
10. **Coordination**: As a Reaction, choose an ally within **2 × Persuasion Mod m**. Coordination cannot be used if your **Persuasion Mod is zero or negative**. You grant them an immediate Reaction: they do not need their own Reaction available and do not spend it. They may instead **reroll their save** against the ability targeting them.

## Attack and Damage Rules

When you attack with a weapon or magical Focus, the success of the attack and the damage inflicted depend on a die roll. The **nature of the die** used (e.g., d6, d8, or d12) is determined by the weapon or Focus you use.

### Hands and Two-Weapon Fighting

- A one-handed weapon occupies one hand; a two-handed weapon occupies both.
- Dual wielding is allowed with **two Light weapons**, or with a normal one-handed weapon and a weapon with the **Secondary** property.
- The Action attacks with the main weapon. The Bonus Action attacks with the secondary weapon.
- When the combination follows the dual-wielding rules, the secondary attack adds its modifier to damage normally.
- Drawing, stowing or switching weapons costs a **Bonus Action**.

### Melee Weapons

- **Damage** : Based generally on **Strength** (e.g.: 1d8 + Strength Mod).
- **Action bonus** : After an attack, you can make a **second attack without adding your modifier to the damage**. (if you dual wield, you can add the modifier)
- **Critical damage** : If you roll the maximum value on a die (e.g.: 8 on 1d8), reroll that die and add the result (cumulative). If the weapon is **Agile**, also reapply the full attack bonus on each critical.
- **Critical miss** : If you roll a **1** on an attack die, you automatically miss. For every **2 points** below **10** in **Agility**, increase your **Miss Threshold** by **1**.
- If you **attack** with **advantage**, roll the die **3** times and take the highest value. On a critical, reroll with **one less advantage**.
- **Opportunity attack** : If an enemy leaves your **Zone of Control** (**1.5 m**), you can make an opportunity attack using your **reaction**. If you **dual wield**, make an attack with each of your weapons.
- You may **Parry** with a melee weapon: **maximum die ÷ 2 + Agility Mod + Deflection**. A shield uses **Strength Mod**. Two weapons add both half-die values, then add Agility Mod and Deflection once.
- **Melee mechanics:** See Actions, Bonus Actions and Reactions for more details.

### Ranged Weapons

- **Damage** : Based generally on **Agility** (e.g.: 1d8 + Agility Mod).
- **Disadvantage in melee** : If you are in an enemy's **Zone of Control**, you have disadvantage on your attack rolls.
- **Critical damage** : If you roll the maximum value on a die (e.g.: 8 on 1d8), reroll that die and add the result (cumulative). If the weapon is **Agile**, also reapply the full attack bonus on each critical.
- **Critical miss** : If you roll a **1** on an attack die, you automatically miss. For every **2 points** below **10** in **Agility**, increase your **Miss Threshold** by **1**.
- **High Ground (bows only)**: when using a bow, you have **advantage** on the attack if you are at least **5 m above** your target.
- **Ranged mechanics:** See Actions, Bonus Actions and Reactions for more details.

### Magic Foci

Magic Foci allow channeling **Spirit** to amplify magical abilities. Each Focus has a **Suit** among **Diamond, Spade, Club, or Heart**.

- **Magic Staff (2 hands)** : 1d8 + Spirit Modifier, grants **+2 Color bonus**.
- **Focus**: 1d4 + Spirit Modifier, **cannot crit**, grants **+1 Color bonus**.
- **Channeling** : during channeling, the Focus creates an **elemental aura** corresponding to its suit. This aura lasts **2 rounds** and applies the **cost reduction** of the Focus's suit calculated according to the formula *(Spirit Modifier + Focus bonus)*. It also grants you **advantage** on **attack** and **ability** rolls with that Focus.
- **Advanced Foci** : Some more powerful Foci offer **higher Color bonus and increased damage**.
- **Double Focus** : If you wield **two one-handed Foci**, you can channel them simultaneously to combine the **Channeling Colors**.
- **Disadvantage in melee** : If you are in an enemy's **Zone of Control**, you have disadvantage on your attack rolls.
- **Critical damage** : If you roll the maximum value on a die (e.g.: 8 on 1d8), reroll that die and add the result (cumulative).
- **Critical miss** : If you roll a **1** on an attack die, you automatically miss. For every **2 points** below **10** in **Agility**, increase your **Miss Threshold** by **1**.

### Making an Attack Roll

1. 🗡️ **Calculate Attack Bonus**
   - **Associated Attribute Modifier**: Strength Mod / Agility Mod / Spirit Mod.
   - **+ Weapon Bonus** (+X).
   - **+ Weapon Perfection Bonus**.
   - **+ All External Bonuses**.
2. 🎲 **Roll the Base Die**
   - Roll **1 weapon die** (d4, d6, d8, d10, d12).
   - If the result is **less than or equal to the Miss Threshold** (generally 1) → **Miss**.
   - If the result is also the die’s maximum value, the **critical takes priority** and the attack succeeds.
3. 💥 **Handle Crits**
   - If the die reaches its **maximum value** → **Crit**.
   - Reroll the die and **add the result** to the sum of dice.
   - If the new die also reaches its maximum value, **repeat**.
   - A 1 rolled on a critical reroll never cancels the attack: it simply adds 1 damage.
4. 🧮 **Calculate Damage**
   - Add **all die results**.
   - Add **once the attack bonus** calculated in step 1.
   - **If Agile** : also add this bonus **for each crit**.

**Final Formula:**

`Damage = Sum of dice + Attack Bonus × (1 + Number of crits if Agile)`

### 🎲 Advantages and Disadvantages

- **Advantage** : +2 dice per advantage (1 = 3 dice, 2 = 5 dice...). Keep the **best**.
- **Disadvantage** : +2 dice per disadvantage (1 = 3 dice, 2 = 5 dice...). Keep the **smallest**.
- **Cancellation** : Advantages and disadvantages cancel each other out one by one.

### 💥 Crits with Advantage

- For each crit, reroll with **one less advantage**.
- Add the kept result to the damage.
- At 0 advantage, continue crits with **1 die**.

**Example:** 3 advantages → Crit → 2 advantages → Crit → 1 advantage → Crit → 0 advantage → Crit → 0 advantage...

### ⚔️ Engagement

During an attack with a **melee weapon**, you can choose to **engage your attack**.

**Bonus**: Add your **Strength or Agility** modifier a second time to the total attack bonus.
- **Disadvantage**: The attack suffers disadvantage.
- **Exposure**: You become **Exposed** after the attack.
- **Agile**: If the weapon has the **Agile** property, you do not become Exposed.

**Narratively**, the engagement can represent a more powerful attack, a targeted strike, or a particularly risky maneuver.

## Combat tokens

Reminder of the rules in [[en/systeme-de-base|Core System]], for reference during combat.

- **Reserve per attribute:** `max(0, 1 + Attribute Mod)`, then any racial bonuses. Tokens reset after a short or long rest, not at the start of a round.
- **Strength:** after a basic attack, or after an ability that uses base damage, allows performing **a second complete attack** (double strike).
- **Agility:** on a basic attack, allows **curving** the attack to hit up to **3 different targets** with the same damage (possible during an uninterrupted movement).
- **Spirit:** on a magical/elemental basic attack, applies a **Reverberation**: at the start of your next turn, calculate **2× the attack’s raw damage, before any reduction**, then apply damage reductions to the Reverberation normally.
- **Social:** without making an attack yourself, have an ally within **20 m** make a **basic attack**. You may modify that attack with another of your combat tokens. The ally may also use their own tokens.

Using a combat token is **free**: it costs no action, bonus action or reaction. You may decide to use it **after the roll**, but its effect must involve a **basic attack**. You may spend at most **1 combat token of each attribute per round**. A single attack may receive at most **1 combat token of each attribute**, regardless of who spends it.

### Combining combat tokens

Tokens combine **multiplicatively**, their effects applying to each other:

- **Strength + Agility** → Two attacks, each able to hit up to 3 targets (×6).
- **Strength + Spirit** → Two attacks, each reverberating (×6).
- **Strength + Social** → An ally performs two attacks.
- **Agility + Spirit** → Three targets, each suffering a reverberation (×9).
- **Agility + Social** → An ally attacks up to 3 targets.
- **Spirit + Social** → The ally's attack is reverberated (×3).

In summary: **Strength** multiplies attacks (×2), **Agility** multiplies targets (up to ×3), **Spirit** multiplies damage (×3 with reverberation), and **Social** transfers the attack to an ally, who benefits from the effects of other tokens.

## Weapons

The table below groups basic weapon categories, with their types and average damage. Weapons you will find in the game may be endowed with enchantments or special effects, but they will always follow this basic skeleton to remain consistent with the system.

### ⚔️ Straight Sword Family — Guard

**Family Property:**

- All straight swords inflict **slashing or piercing** (choice at each attack).
- Straight swords use the standard Parry formula with **Agility Mod**.
- If your Parry reduces at least some of a melee attack’s damage, you have **advantage on your next attack** against that assailant. A perfect parry also grants this advantage; it stacks with the shield’s perfect-parry benefit if that benefit’s conditions are met.

**Weapons:**

- **Dagger** — 1d4, slashing/piercing — Agile, Light, Throw (10m)
- **Shortsword** — 1d6, slashing/piercing — Agile, Light
- **Longsword** — 1d8, slashing/piercing — Versatile (1d10)
- **Rapier (greatsword)** — 1d12, slashing/piercing — Two Hands

### 🗡️ Curved Sword Family — Fluid

**Family Property:**

- All curved swords inflict **slashing**.
- **Offensive Dance**: If you attack with **disadvantage**, you have **advantage** on your next attack; you can choose to attack with **disadvantage**.
- **Uninterrupted Dance**: When you attack a creature, it has **disadvantage** on its opportunity attacks against you until the end of the round.

**Weapons:**

- **Sickle** — 1d4, slashing — Agile, Light
- **Curved Sword (scimitar)** — 1d6, slashing — Agile, Light
- **Katana (long saber)** — 1d10, slashing — Agile, Two Hands
- **Great Saber** — 1d12, slashing — Two Hands

### 🪓 Axe Family — Brutality

**Family Property:**

- All weapons in the axe family inflict **slashing**.
- **Brutality**: On a critical hit, roll exactly **2 damage dice**, without advantage, and keep their sum. This reroll produces another critical only when both dice form a **pair**: roll two more dice without advantage and add their sum to the damage. Repeat as long as you roll a pair.

**Weapons:**

- **Hand Axe** — 1d6, slashing — Light, Throw (10m)
- **Battle Axe** — 1d8, slashing — Versatile (1d10)
- **Great Axe** — 1d12, slashing — Two Hands

### 🔨 Bludgeon Family — Impact

**Family Property:**

- All impact weapons inflict **bludgeoning**.
- **Impact**: Ignores passive armor and halves Parry.
- On a critical hit, the attacker makes an **Athletics check against the target’s Strength**. On a success, they push the target back **5 m**.

**Weapons:**

- **Club** — 1d4, bludgeoning — Improvised, Brute
- **Combat Gauntlet** — 1d6, bludgeoning — Light
- **Warhammer** — 1d8, bludgeoning — Versatile (1d10)
- **Great Mace** — 1d12, bludgeoning — Two Hands

### 🪶 Reach Weapons Family — Reach

**Family Property:**

- Range of **3m**.
- You can make an opportunity attack if a creature **leaves your extended zone of control of 3m**.
- You can also make one if a creature **enters your restricted zone of 1.5m**.

**Weapons:**

- **Long Staff** — 1d6, bludgeoning — Amplitude, Reach
- **Spear** — 1d8, piercing — Versatile (1d10), Amplitude, Reach
- **Halberd** — 1d12, slashing — Two Hands, Reach

### 🏹 Bow Family

**Common Property: Overreach (advantage when shooting from elevation ≥ 5m).**

- **Shortbow** — 1d6, piercing — Distance, Overreach
- **Longbow** — 1d8, piercing — Distance, Overreach

### 🔫 Crossbow Family

**Common Property: Piercing (ignores passive armor, divides Parry).**

*(Crossbows always start combat already reloaded.)*

- **Hand Crossbow** — 1d4, piercing — Distance, Secondary, Piercing, Brute, Light Shot
- **Crossbow** (classic/light) — 1d8, piercing — Distance, Piercing
- **Heavy Crossbow** — 1d12, piercing — Distance, Piercing, **Recharge** (bonus action to reload, action to shoot)

### 🔮 Focus Family → Focus

**Family Property:**

- **Ranged** weapon.
- Uses **Spirit** for damage rolls.
- Used for **Channeling** (adds the Channeling characteristic defined by the system).

**Weapons:**

- **Focus** — 1d4 — Focus, Secondary, Brute, Light Shot
- **Staff** — 1d8 — Focus, Two-Hands

### 🛡️ Shield Family → Bastion

**Family Property:**

Offers **passive armor** independent of Strength and a **customized Parry value**. The **weapon bonuses** also apply to the shield. Using **Bastion** with a shield automatically **parries**. A **perfect parry** means parrying all damage. With a shield, a perfect parry in melee allows you to make an **Athletics check against the opponent’s Strength**; on a success, the opponent is knocked **Prone**. You use **Strength** instead of **Agility** to parry and need at least **10 in Strength** to equip it.

**Weapons:**

- **Shield** — 1d4 bludgeoning — Brute, Secondary, Bastion

### 🗡️ Unique Weapons

#### 🪓⚔️ Scythe — Fluid + Reach

**Profile**: 1d10, slashing — Two Hands, Reach (3m), Amplitude

- Inherits **Fluid** (curved swords) and **Reach** properties.
- → Hybrid weapon combining **mobility** and **zone control**.

#### ⚔️ Rapier — Counter-Thrust

**Profile**: 1d8, piercing — Agile

- **Counter-Thrust**: When a creature attacks you in melee, you can choose to attempt a counter.
    - If you do, that creature's attack is made with **advantage**.
    - You then apply your **Parry** normally.
    - After parrying, you can immediately make **a free attack** against that creature, even if your Parry only cancels part of the damage and you take the remaining damage.

#### 🌀 Whip — Soft Entanglement

**Profile**: 1d6, slashing — Agile, Reach (3m)

- **Soft Entanglement**: Your **opportunity attacks** with a whip are made with **disadvantage**.
- If they succeed, you make an **Sleight of Hand** check opposed to the target's **Athletics**.
- On success, the target is put **on the ground (prone)**.

#### ✊ Fist Weapons — Hybrid

Profile: 1d4, bludgeoning

- **Impact**: Benefit from the Impact property.
- **Hybrid**: Add your Strength and Agility modifiers to the initial attack bonus, along with all other attack bonuses. As an exception to the general rule, on every critical, reroll the die and reapply the attack bonus, excluding only Strength Mod. These weapons are **not Agile**: **Engagement** therefore makes you **Exposed**.

#### 🔗 Flail — Destabilization

**Profile** : 1d8, piercing — Special

- **Destabilization** : if you **crit** with a flail, the target has **disadvantage on their next attack**.

### 📜 List of Properties

#### ⚔️ Basic Properties

- **Agile** : uses either Strength Mod or Agility Mod for attack and damage rolls. On every critical, reapply the full attack bonus.
- **Light** : can be used for a two-weapon attack.
- **Versatile (dX)** : can be used one-handed or two-handed (increased damage).
- **Two-handed** : must be wielded with two hands.
- **Throw (Xm)** : can be thrown at range, range indicated.
- **Range** : ranged weapon, ranged attack, must use Agility.
- **Improvised** : makeshift weapon.
- **Brute** : the weapon cannot inflict critical hits.
- **Light Shot** : can shoot as a bonus action or action but not both (ranged weapons).
- **Reload** : requires a bonus action to reload before shooting (starts reloaded).
- **Secondary**: Can be equipped on the secondary hand in addition to a non-light weapon.
- **Amplitude** : When you attack a creature at your maximum range (3 m), your weapon gains the Agile property.

#### 🏷️ Family Properties

- **Guard (Straight Swords)** : Swords deal slashing or piercing damage. A Parry reducing at least some of a melee attack’s damage grants advantage on the next attack against the attacker. A perfect parry also grants this advantage, stacking with the shield’s perfect-parry benefit if its conditions are met.
- **Brutality (Axes)** : Axes deal slashing damage. On a critical, roll exactly two dice without advantage and keep their sum; only a pair produces another critical. On each pair, roll two more dice without advantage and add their sum to the damage; repeat as long as you roll a pair.
- **Impact (Maces/Hammers)** : Impact weapons deal bludgeoning damage, ignore passive Armor and halve Parry. On a critical hit, the attacker makes an Athletics check against the target’s Strength; on a success, the target is pushed back 5 m.
- **Fluid (Curved Swords)** : Curved swords deal slashing damage; if they attack with disadvantage, they have advantage on the next attack in the round, and any target attacked has disadvantage on opportunity attacks against you until the end of the round.
- **Reach (Staves, Spears, Halberds)** : These weapons strike at 3 m, can make an opportunity attack when a creature leaves their 3 m zone or when it enters their 1.5 m zone.
- **High Ground (Bows)**: If the shooter is located at ≥ 5 m above their target, they gain advantage on the attack roll.
- **Piercing (Crossbows)** : The attack ignores passive Armor and halves the target's Parry value.
- **Bulwark (Shields)**: Grants passive Armor independent of Strength and a dedicated Parry; weapon bonuses apply, **Bastion** makes parry, and a **perfect parry** (all damage parried) in melee allows an **Athletics check against the opponent’s Strength** to knock them **Prone** on a success.

#### 🌀 Unique Properties

- **Counter-Thrust (Rapier)** : When you are attacked in melee, you can grant advantage to the enemy, then parry normally, and finally immediately perform a free rapier attack against them, even if the Parry is partial and you take the remaining damage.
- **Entanglement (Whip)** : Opportunity attacks with a whip are made with disadvantage, but if they hit, you make an Sleight of Hand save against the target's Athletics to knock them down.
- **Destabilization (Flail)** : When you inflict a critical hit with a flail, the target has disadvantage on their next attack.
- **Hybrid** : Add Strength Mod and Agility Mod to the initial attack bonus, along with all other attack bonuses. As an exception to the general rule, every critical rerolls the die and reapplies this bonus, excluding only Strength Mod. This property does not grant **Agile**: **Engagement** therefore makes you **Exposed**.

## Armors

Armor offers passive damage reduction and a contribution to Parry. It has two distinct values:

- **Armor** 🛡️ (*capped by Strength Mod + 1*) : passively reduces damage remaining after Parry.
- **Deflection** 🔄 (*Adds to Parry*) : Used only during a **Parry** to improve active defense.

**General Rules:**

1. Apply **immunities, resistances and vulnerabilities** first.
2. Apply **Parry** next.
3. Apply **Armor** to the remaining damage last.
4. Worn Armor is capped at **Strength Mod + 1**.
5. Armor granted by a **shield** or a **racial natural-armor bonus** is added and ignores the Strength Mod + 1 cap.
6. Total Armor reduction can never exceed **50% of the damage remaining after Parry**, rounded down.

Example: an attack deals 10 damage and Parry removes 6. Four damage remains, so Armor can reduce at most 2.

- **Minimum of 10 in Strength required to wear a breastplate with armor.**

### Armor Equipment

A complete armor consists of four main pieces.

Each offers **Deflection**, and some confer an additional attribute influencing the wearer's combat style.

| Equipment | Main Values | Secondary Attribute | Short Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Plastron** 🦾 | Déflexion | **Armor** | Central protection piece. Sole source of **Passive Armor**, it directly absorbs part of the damage. |
| **Casque** 🪖 | Déflexion | **Willpower** 🧠 | Protects the head and strengthens **concentration**. Offers +X to all resilience saving throws against loss of concentration. |
| **Bottes** 🥾 | Déflexion | **Speed** 🌀 | Improve mobility. Offer +X to **movement speed**. |
| **Gantelets** ✊ | Déflexion | **Initiative** ⚡ | Favorize reactivity. Offer +X to the wearer's **initiative**. |

## Unconsciousness and Fatigue

Being unconscious does not mean death, but you are vulnerable. Taking damage in this state causes you to accumulate fatigue levels, which can lead to death if no one intervenes.

### Rules

1. **Falling Unconscious:**
    - HP can never fall below **0**. When dropping to 0 HP, gain **1 Fatigue level**.
    - For every attack or damage tick suffered at 0 HP, make a **Resilience** check against half the damage taken, rounded down. On a failure, gain **1 Fatigue level**.
    - At **3 Fatigue levels**, the character dies immediately.
    - You lose fatigue levels by resting.
2. **Stabilization by an ally:**
    - An ally uses their **Bonus Action**. You return to **1 HP**, keep your Fatigue, and retain only resources you had not already spent this round. Stabilization does not refresh any resource.
3. **Healing:**
    - Receiving healing restores consciousness and the stated amount of HP.
4. **Self-recovery:**
    - **Explicit exception to Downed and its prohibition on acting:** at 0 HP, you may spend your **available Action** solely to attempt self-recovery, making a Resilience check against `5 + 5 × your Fatigue level`. This attempt grants no additional Action: if your Action is already spent, you must wait until it next becomes available. The Action is spent even if the check fails.
    - On a success, you return to **1 HP**. This recovery adds no Fatigue level.

### Fatigue Effects

Each Fatigue level reduces the value of all your cards by 1, including crystallized cards. A card is always worth at least **1**, because any card may be used as a 1 of any suit.

| **Level** | **Effects** |
| --- | --- |
| **0** | No penalty. |
| **1** | Every card you use is worth **1 less**. |
| **2** | Every card you use is worth **2 less**. |
| **3** | Death. |

## ⚔️ Damage Types

**Weapons**, **artifacts**, and **abilities** inflict **damage** of different natures depending on their origin or energy.

Each damage type describes the **way the attack interacts with matter, energy, or spirit**.

Certain resistances, vulnerabilities, or elemental synergies depend directly on this typology.

### Resistances and Vulnerabilities

- **Minor Resistance**: attacks using this damage type have **disadvantage**.
- **Major Resistance**: damage of this type is **divided by 2**.
- **Resistance with no specified level**: resistance is **major** by default; minor resistance must be explicitly identified as such.
- **Minor Vulnerability**: attacks using this damage type gain **advantage**.
- **Major Vulnerability**: damage of this type is **multiplied by 2**.
- **Immunity**: damage of this type is reduced to **0**.
- **Stacking for the same damage type**: two minor resistances combine into one major resistance. Multiple major resistances do not stack: damage is divided by 2 only once. Major and minor resistance to the same damage type coexist: the attack has disadvantage, then damage is divided by 2. The same logic applies to vulnerabilities: two minor vulnerabilities become one major vulnerability, multiple major vulnerabilities multiply damage by 2 only once, and a major vulnerability alongside a minor vulnerability grants advantage on the attack, then doubles damage.
- Apply these effects **before Parry and Armor**. A resistance and vulnerability of the same level cancel each other. If they have different levels, apply them independently: major resistance and minor vulnerability grant advantage on the attack, then divide damage by 2; minor resistance and major vulnerability impose disadvantage on the attack, then multiply damage by 2.

Damage is grouped into three main families:

1. **Physical** — related to strength, matter, and movement.
2. **Magical** — resulting from the manipulation of pure or spiritual energy.
3. **Elemental** — direct expression of natural and alchemical forces.

### 🪓 1. Physical Damage

Physical damage comes from a **tangible attack**, exerted by a projectile, weapon, or impact.

They are the direct result of the **speed, mass**, or **precision** of the attacker.

| Type | Description | Examples of weapons or effects |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Bludgeoning** | Massive shock crushing or projecting the target. | Maces, hammers, fists, shockwave explosions. |
| **Slashing** | Cuts or gashes by force or speed. | Swords, axes, claws, energy blades. |
| **Piercing** | Penetrates or pierces by concentration of force. | Spears, arrows, darts, magical points. |

➡️ These three forms cover all damage related to **solid matter or kinetic energy**.

### 🔮 2. Magical Damage

Magical damage results from a **direct manipulation of arcane, spiritual, or mental fluxes**.

They ignore physical laws and translate how magic interacts with the very essence of beings and things.

| Type | **Description** | **Effect** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Psychic** | Mental and emotional damage affecting consciousness, will, and perception directly. | Ignores only Parry and Armor. Immunities, resistances and vulnerabilities apply normally. |
| **Necrotic** | Vital damage stemming from corruption, entropy, and the decomposition of internal energy. | Negates the target’s minor and major resistances for 1 round, except its resistances to necrotic damage. Immunities remain unaffected. A target with minor or major resistance to necrotic damage suffers no negation of any of its resistances. |
| **Radiant** | Luminescent and energetic damage amplifying the magical resonance of surrounding elements. | Grants advantage on Nature-Survival checks and elemental-combination damage rolls. |
| **Force** | Pure magical pressure damage, condensed stabilized kinetic energy. | Propagates elemental conditions within a 5 m radius without a save. |
| **Arcane** | Raw magic damage, unaffiliated with an element or school, representing pure mana force. | There is no resistance to this type of damage. |

### 🌋 3. Elemental Damage

Elemental damage expresses the **violence or reaction of natural forces**. Its types are **Fire, Ice, Water, Lightning and Toxic**. Acid and poison effects both use the **Toxic** damage type; cold effects use the **Ice** damage type. They are not separate damage types.

They stem from alchemical magics, natural spells, or unstable environments.

| Type | Description | Examples of Effects |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Fire** | Consumes and burns, destroys organic matter, and propagates heat. | Flames, explosions, incandescent projectiles. |
| **Ice** | Freezes and slows, solidifies surfaces, and causes frostbite. | Frost trails, snowstorms, frozen breaths. |
| **Water** | Crushes, sweeps away, or drowns; can conduct or dissipate other energies. | Jets, waves, magical rains. |
| **Lightning** | Pure unstable energy, traverses bodies and metals. | Lightning bolts, electrical arcs, magnetic shocks. |
| **Toxic** | Gases, venoms, and acids; poisons, corrodes, or infects. | Alchemical mists, poisons, corrosive exudates. |

➡️ Elements often interact with each other: water conducts lightning, fire extinguishes in water, poison ignites, etc.

### 🌐 Elemental Interactions

All elemental damage automatically applies its corresponding condition (**Burning**, **Chilled**, **Soaked**, **Shocked**, or **Poisoned**) until the end of the round. Reapplying the same element only renews this duration. These conditions serve elemental interactions and combinations; they cause no ongoing damage, slowing or other additional effect unless explicitly stated by a rule or ability.

When a different element is applied to an already affected target, the new element’s damage is amplified or reduced according to the compatibility between both forces. After resolution, the new condition replaces the previous one unless a combination is activated.

This table represents the **fundamental elemental synergies and counter-reactions** of the system.

It applies whenever an elemental effect is inflicted **on a target already under the influence of another element**.

#### ⚙️ Application Rule

- These interactions affect only the **damage** of the **second element applied**. The table’s advantage or disadvantage still applies to the initial attack’s damage even when Alteration triggers an elemental combination.
- **Advantage:** the caster rolls their damage dice with advantage.
- **Disadvantage:** the caster rolls their damage dice with disadvantage.
- **Neutral:** no particular effect.
- Effects are **symmetric**: the relationship is identical in both directions.

## 🌩️ Elemental Combinations

**Elemental combinations** represent direct reactions between two opposing or complementary elements.

They allow **temporarily altering** the energies present on the battlefield to create a unique effect: explosion, propagation, terrain transformation, etc.

These interactions are **controlled by the player**, via the **Alteration** reaction.

### ⚙️ Activating a Combination

#### 🔸 Alteration (Reaction)

The caster **uses their reaction** to alter **their own spell or elemental attack**. They trigger a **corresponding elemental reaction** that makes sense involving the element they apply and the element already applied on the target or the place where it is cast.

Activating the combination **consumes both elemental conditions involved, even if the contextual check fails**.

#### 🔹 Conditions

- You can alter or alter **your own abilities** or those of your **allies**, but **never directly those of enemies.**
- A **contextual check** is required to determine how the elemental combination can be used in the situation. Make this check **before resolving the initial attack’s damage**.
    - Successful roll → harmonized effect, stable combination.
    - Failed roll → reduced or uncontrolled effect, at the GM's choice.

### ⚖️ Safeguards and Limits

| Category | Limit / Rule | Detail |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Control** | ❌ No hard CC | No daze, paralysis, complete freeze, etc. |
| **Damage** | 🧭 Raw Power (Spirit Mod d6) | Combination damage equals **Raw Power**, meaning the caster’s **Spirit Mod d6**. It is added to the initial damage. |
| **Propagation** | 🌐Max 5m Zone | Fixed radius of 5 meters around the impact point. |
| **Transformation** | 🎲Contextual roll required | The GM decides the difficulty based on coherence and narration. |
| **Prolongation** | ⏳2 rounds | If the effect persists, it deals **50% of the initial damage** each round for **2 rounds**. |
| **Disturbance** | 🌫Moderate | Slightly disturbs (smoke, steam, light) without unbalancing combat. |

## Invisibility

- Invisibility has no fixed duration: it lasts until a rule ends it.
- An enemy that detects you can attack you normally, without disadvantage from your Invisibility. This detection does not end your Invisibility toward other enemies.
- If you become Invisible outside every enemy's field of vision, no Perception check is made when it activates.
- When you use an ability that affects no enemy, each enemy may make a **Perception check against your Stealth**, with disadvantage because you are Invisible. An enemy that succeeds detects you.
- An ability that affects an enemy automatically ends your Invisibility.
- If you move within **5 m** of an enemy, that enemy may make a **Perception check against your Stealth** with disadvantage. On a success, they detect you.

## Negative Conditions

Unless its description gives another duration or an exit procedure, a condition lasts **1 round**, then ends automatically.

- Reapplying the same condition **refreshes its duration** without stacking its effects.
- When effects conflict, a **prohibition overrides a permission**.
- Opposing automatic success and automatic failure **cancel each other**: make the roll normally.

## 📑 NPC Action Types

### 1. Action

- Each NPC has **one Action per turn**.
- Used to attack, cast a spell, or use a primary ability.
- *Example: the orc uses its Action to strike with an axe.*

### 2. Bonus Action

- Some NPCs have **one Bonus Action per turn**.
- Used for quick, secondary, or support abilities.
- *Example: the warlord uses its Bonus Action to give an order to its minions.*

### 3. Reaction

- Each NPC has **one Reaction per global round**.
- Used when a trigger occurs (often outside its turn).
- *Example: the guard uses its Reaction to attack when an enemy leaves its range.*

### 4. Legendary Actions

- Some bosses have **1 to 3 Legendary Actions per global round**.
- Triggered at the end of another creature's turn.
- Provide more rhythm and prevent a boss from being passive.
- *Example: the dragon uses a Legendary Action to deliver a tail slap after a player's turn.*

### 5. Legendary Reactions

- Special reactions available only for certain bosses.
- Triggered like a normal Reaction, but consuming a legendary resource.
- *Example: the demon uses a Legendary Reaction to counter a spell cast against it.*

### 6. Lair Actions

- Bosses fighting in their lair have **one Lair Action per round**.
- Triggered on initiative count 20 (after adjustment).
- Represents the power of their environment or domain.
- *Example: the lich uses a Lair Action to summon shadows from its floor.*

### 7. Scripted Actions

- Some NPCs automatically trigger actions at a **HP threshold**, a **specific round**, or a **narrative event**.
- Do not count in their normal action economy.
- *Example: when the golem drops to 50% HP, its plates open and release a burst of flames.*

## ⚔️ Legendary Break

- A boss has a **limited number of Legendary Breaks per combat**.
- **2-Turn Constraint**: an effect must normally remain active for at least **2 turns belonging to any creatures or players** before it can be broken.
- If the creature has not yet taken its turn when the effect is applied, it spends its Legendary Break **at the end of the round**, regardless of how many turns have passed.
- If it has already taken its turn during that round, it may spend its Legendary Break **during the next round, before taking its turn**.
- The creature may use its **Reaction** to spend a Legendary Break immediately and break the effect.
